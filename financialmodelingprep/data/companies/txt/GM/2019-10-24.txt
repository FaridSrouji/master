UAW AGREES TO CONTRACT WITH GM, BUT KC ISN'T IN THE CLEAR. THE UNITED AUTO WORKERS ANNOUNCED THAT ITS MEMBERS HAD VOTED TO APPROVE A NEW CONTRACT WITH GENERAL MOTORS CO., ENDING THE COMPANY'S LONGEST STRIKE IN 50 YEARS. THE VOTE MEANS THAT WORK WILL BEGIN AGAIN AT GM'S FAIRFAX ASSEMBLY PLANT IN KANSAS CITY, KAN. - AND FOR VARIOUS SUPPLIERS IN THE AREA THAT SUPPORT THE PLANT. THE KC-AREA ECONOMY LOST $20.4 MILLION A WEEK WHILE THE STRIKE WAS ON, ACCORDING TO ESTIMATES BY THE CENTER FOR AUTOMOTIVE RESEARCH. AN ANNOUNCEMENT POSTED ON THE UAW WEBSITE CONFIRMED THE END OF THE STRIKE, BUT DID NOT INCLUDE INFORMATION ON THE MARGIN OF THE VOTE. OFFICIALS WITH UAW LOCAL 31, WHICH REPRESENTS THE LOCAL GM PLANT, COULD NOT IMMEDIATELY BE REACHED FOR COMMENT. THE CONTRACT INCLUDES AN $11,000 SIGNING BONUS, PERFORMANCE BONUSES, TWO 3% ANNUAL RAISES AND TWO 4% LUMP-SUM PAYMENTS, ACCORDING TO THE UAW WEBSITE. BUT THE END OF THE GM STRIKE DOESN'T MEAN THE KANSAS CITY AREA IS IN THE CLEAR. THE UNION NOW WILL USE THE GM AGREEMENT AS A TEMPLATE FOR NEGOTIATIONS WITH FORD MOTOR CO., WHOSE KANSAS CITY ASSEMBLY PLANT IN CLAYCOMO EMPLOYS ROUGHLY 6,600 - MAKING IT THE LARGEST MANUFACTURER IN THE AREA. THE GM FAIRFAX ASSEMBLY & STAMPING PLANT EMPLOYS 2,177.
